## Sky — epigraph "Looking up."

The sky is the one subject that will not hold still to be photographed. It
assembles and disbands on its own clock, and the whole of the work is to be
standing under it, attending, in the few minutes it becomes what it is going
to become. These frames are not of clouds. They are of light deciding what to
do with them.

## Earth — epigraph "Looking close."

The ground rewards the opposite of ambition. Nothing here is far away or hard
to reach; it is a leaf, a stone, a surface most people walk over. The
photograph is the noticing, held long enough that a thing too small to
announce itself gets to be seen at the scale it deserves.

## Water — epigraph "Where land dissolves."

Water is where the edge of a place stops being certain. These are made at the
thresholds, the tide line and the far shore and the fog that takes the
horizon out, where the eye cannot say exactly where the land ends. The
subject is not the water. It is the dissolving.

## Structure — epigraph "What we build toward the sky."

Everything in this series was built by someone who wanted it to last. I
photograph it the way the light finds it, not the way the drawings promised
it, so the surface reads as something aged and held rather than rendered. A
building is a claim about permanence. The photograph is the weather answering.

## Light — epigraph "What changes everything."

This is the series about the material itself. Not what the light falls on,
but the light, treated as the subject and not the illumination. Every other
category is a place; this one is the thing all of them are actually made of,
photographed the few times it became visible on its own terms.
